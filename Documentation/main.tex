\section{Introduction and Background}
% ============================================================

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is slow because each axial slice is acquired
sequentially. Long scans reduce patient comfort, raise costs, and limit
scanner throughput. A natural acceleration strategy is to acquire only every
$k$-th slice along the slice-encoding axis and reconstruct the missing ones
in software. We study $k{=}2$ (50\,\% of slices missing) and frame
reconstruction as image-to-image regression: predict the missing slice
$\hat{y}$ from its two acquired neighbours $(x_{\text{left}}, x_{\text{right}})$
(2.5D framing).

Classical interpolation (linear, cubic spline) handles this in closed form
but blurs sharp anatomical edges. Convolutional networks --- in particular
the U-Net architecture~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} --- have become the
standard for medical image-to-image tasks because their skip connections
preserve fine spatial detail through the bottleneck. We extend this baseline
with a controlled \textbf{ablation} (PlainCNN, identical architecture
\emph{without} skip connections), \textbf{cross-dataset} validation on
BraTS2020~\cite{bakas2017brats}, and four complementary
\textbf{explainability} methods: Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam},
Integrated Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017ig}, occlusion
sensitivity~\cite{zeiler2014visualizing}, and MC-Dropout
uncertainty~\cite{gal2016dropout}. Reconstruction quality is evaluated with
PSNR, SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} and MAE.

% ============================================================
\section{Method}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Datasets}
\textbf{IXI} (primary): $\sim$600 brain MRI scans from healthy volunteers
acquired at three London hospitals on different scanners and field
strengths~\cite{ixi}. Only T1-weighted MPRAGE sequences are used; 581 scans
are retained after quality filtering, producing \textbf{42\,816
input--target pairs} split 70/15/15 (seed 42). \textbf{BraTS2020}
(cross-dataset)~\cite{bakas2017brats}: 57\,195 pre-extracted axial HDF5
slices, T1 channel, skull-stripped, including tumour-bearing scans; the
same 2.5D construction yields \textbf{28\,413 pairs}.

\subsection{Model Architecture}
The reconstruction model is a \textbf{U-Net}~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} with
four encoder stages (channels $[32,64,128,256]$), 512-channel bottleneck,
and a mirrored decoder with bilinear upsampling and concatenated skip
connections at every resolution (\textbf{7.76\,M parameters}; final
\texttt{Sigmoid} constrains outputs to $[0,1]$). Each stage is two
\texttt{Conv3$\times$3 $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU} blocks; downsampling is
\texttt{MaxPool2d(2)}.

\textbf{PlainCNN ablation.} Identical depth, widths, blocks and parameter
count, but skip connections are \emph{removed}. This isolates the
architectural contribution of skips from confounders such as capacity or
depth.

\textbf{Classical baselines.} Linear interpolation (pixel-wise
position-weighted average of neighbours) and cubic spline (fit through all
acquired slices along depth, evaluated at missing positions).

\subsection{Training Setup}
\begin{itemize}
    \itemsep0.2em
    \item \textbf{Preprocessing}: per-volume min--max normalisation to
          $[0,1]$, bicubic resize to $256\times256$ with anti-aliasing.
          Under $\times2$ acceleration, every second slice is discarded;
          for each missing slice the model receives its two acquired
          neighbours as a 2-channel input.
    \item \textbf{Loss}: hybrid
          $\mathcal{L}=0.8\,\lVert\hat{y}-y\rVert_{1}+0.2\,(1-\mathrm{SSIM}(\hat{y},y))$.
    \item \textbf{Optimiser}: Adam, lr $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$,
          \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} (patience 5, factor 0.5).
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameters}: batch size 8, up to 20 epochs,
          best-validation checkpoint kept, RTX 5070 Ti.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation Details}
Preprocessed arrays are stored as three-phase NumPy memmaps (count,
allocate, fill) to avoid loading the $\approx$22\,GB IXI pair set into RAM.
Explainability is run on 50 uniformly-sampled test cases:
Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} on the final decoder block; Integrated
Gradients~\cite{sundararajan2017ig} with 50 path steps and a zero baseline;
slice-channel ablation (zero one input slice and measure output change);
occlusion sensitivity~\cite{zeiler2014visualizing} with a $16{\times}16$
patch slid over each input. Predictive uncertainty uses
MC-Dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout}: a forward-hook dropout ($p{=}0.3$) is
attached to the bottleneck and 20 stochastic passes are averaged. For BraTS
we additionally report \textbf{FG-MAE}, the MAE on foreground voxels
($y>0.02$), because skull-stripped volumes are 70--80\,\% exact zeros that
trivialise raw PSNR (see Appendix~\ref{app:fgmae}).

% ============================================================
\section{Results}
% ============================================================

The U-Net beats both classical baselines by \textbf{+2.86\,dB} PSNR and
$+0.04$ SSIM (Table~\ref{tab:ixi}). Crucially, the PlainCNN ablation
(33.06\,dB) is \emph{worse than linear interpolation} (33.21\,dB):
without skip connections, all spatial information must survive a
$16\times16$ bottleneck, which is too coarse for $256\times256$
reconstruction. Skip connections account for the entire $\sim$3\,dB margin.

\textbf{Cross-dataset generalisation.} The same architecture, retrained on
BraTS pairs, attains \textbf{FG-MAE 0.00754} on tissue voxels (raw PSNR
58.65\,dB but inflated by background, see Appendix~\ref{app:fgmae}). The
model generalises to a structurally different dataset (tumour-bearing,
skull-stripped) without any architectural change.

\textbf{Explainability.} Three independent input-attribution methods (IG,
channel ablation, occlusion) give Left/Right ratios within $\sim$6\,\% of
unity (Table~\ref{tab:expl}), confirming a genuinely \emph{bidirectional}
interpolation. Channel ablation produces large output changes ($\sim 0.08$)
while patch occlusion produces tiny changes ($\sim 3\!\times\!10^{-4}$),
indicating reliance on \emph{global} slice structure rather than local
features --- consistent with anatomical interpolation. Grad-CAM scales with
reconstruction difficulty: hard cases produce diffuse attention, medium
cases focus on the skull boundary and tissue edges, easy boundary slices
show near-zero activation. MC-Dropout variance is essentially zero on the
in-distribution test set, meaning any uncertainty threshold becomes
informative only on out-of-distribution inputs.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

We trained a U-Net for $\times2$ slice-interpolation MRI reconstruction
that achieves 36.07\,dB PSNR on IXI ($+2.86$\,dB over linear interpolation)
and generalises to BraTS2020 (FG-MAE 0.0075) without architectural
modification. The strongest finding is the \textbf{ablation}: the same
network without skip connections is \emph{worse} than 4-tap linear
interpolation, so the +3\,dB skip-connection gain is the difference between
useful and useless --- not an incremental improvement. Four explainability
methods consistently show the model uses both input slices symmetrically,
which is the correct behaviour for an interpolation task.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) IXI contains only healthy volunteers; the IXI
model has never seen tumours or lesions (BraTS partially addresses this
but does not replace a clinical study). (ii) PSNR/SSIM measure average
quality and underweight small focal abnormalities. (iii) No radiologist
reading study has been performed.

\textbf{Possible improvements.} (a) Adaptive acquisition: acquire every
second slice, run anomaly detection, reconstruct only if no findings;
otherwise complete the scan. (b) Uncertainty-gated safety thresholds
calibrated on out-of-distribution data. (c) Pathological validation with
paired healthy/pathological scans and lesion annotations. (d) DTI
integration correlating reconstruction errors with white-matter tracts.

\section{FG-MAE and metric inflation in skull-stripped data}
\label{app:fgmae}
A skull-stripped BraTS slice contains 70--80\,\% exact-zero background.
Both prediction and ground truth equal zero in those regions, contributing
zero squared error. Raw PSNR is dominated by the few non-zero foreground
pixels but normalised by the total pixel count, producing values
($\approx 58$\,dB) that look near-perfect. We therefore mask
ground-truth voxels with $y>0.02$ and report
\[
\mathrm{FG\text{-}MAE} = \frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{(i,j)\in M}\bigl|\hat{y}_{ij}-y_{ij}\bigr|,
\quad M=\{(i,j)\,:\, y_{ij}>0.02\}.
\]
This restricts the average to anatomically meaningful voxels and gives an
honest comparison across datasets.

\section{Loss-weight sensitivity}
The loss mixing coefficient $\alpha$ in
$\mathcal{L}=\alpha\lVert\hat{y}-y\rVert_{1}+(1-\alpha)(1-\mathrm{SSIM})$
was selected on a preliminary sweep. $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.7,0.8,0.9\}$
produced PSNR within $\pm 0.4$\,dB of the chosen value, with $\alpha{=}0.8$
giving the best joint SSIM/PSNR trade-off; the network is robust to this
choice.
